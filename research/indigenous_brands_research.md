# Indigenous-Owned Canadian Consumer Brands — Substitutes for Tariffed US Imports

Research compiled for the zeddays.org "buy Canadian" reference site. Every brand below has (1) Indigenous ownership confirmed from an official or authoritative source, and (2) an identifiable consumer product sold in Canada. Sources are linked inline at the point of each claim. Brands considered but not verifiable are listed in the **Rejected / unverified** section at the end.

Sector labels map to the site taxonomy:
`Wood, pulp and paper` · `Pantry, bakery, snacks, beverages, frozen and canned foods` · `Clothing, footwear and textiles` · `Beer, wine and spirits` · `Cleaning, beauty and personal care` · `Dairy and refrigerated foods`

**Note on Manitobah Mukluks, Nk'Mip Cellars and Cheekbone Beauty:** already listed on the site, so excluded from the count below. (For reference, Cheekbone's own About page states it is "one of the first Canadian Indigenous-owned and founded cosmetics companies," founded by Jenn Harper and headquartered in St. Catharines, Ontario — [Cheekbone Beauty](https://www.cheekbonebeauty.com/pages/about).)

## 1. Wood, pulp and paper products

Softwood lumber is the single longest-running Canada–US trade dispute, and US-origin lumber, plywood, engineered wood and firewood are the substitutable categories here. Two of these companies (Kiskatinaw, Tintina) sell directly to households; the sawmills sell dimensional lumber through the same channels that stock US-origin lumber.

### NorSask Forest Products LP
- **Products:** High-quality SPF (spruce-pine-fir) stud lumber, sold in Canada, the US and overseas.
- **Ownership:** "NorSask Forest Products is owned by the Meadow Lake Tribal Council (MLTC) and operates in Meadow Lake, Saskatchewan… Not only is it the largest First Nations-owned sawmill in Canada, profits generated support economic development, social programs, employment and infrastructure in the nine First Nations communities represented in MLTC." MLTC "acquired 100% ownership of NorSask" in 1998 — [NorSask Forest Products, About Us](https://norsask.ca/about-us/).
- **Location:** Meadow Lake, Saskatchewan (Meadow Lake Tribal Council, nine member First Nations).
- **Substitute for:** US-origin SPF/stud lumber and framing lumber for construction and DIY.
- **Trade framing:** The company's own About page notes its lumber is sold into Canada, the United States and overseas — i.e. it is a direct competitor in the same softwood market that the Canada–US lumber dispute covers.

### Papasay Value-Added Wood Products
- **Products:** Rough-cut cedar lumber, rough-cut SPF softwood lumber, custom-cut lumber up to 6"×6" and 16' long, dimensional lumber, dunnage and blocks; a planer/moulder line for finished products was slated to come online in early 2026.
- **Ownership:** "Papasay Value-Added Wood Products (PVAWP) is a 100% Indigenous-owned sawmill located in Northwestern Ontario, operated by the Bingwi Neyaashi Anishinaabek (Sand Point First Nation)" — [Papasay Value-Added Wood Products](https://www.linkedin.com/company/papasay-value-added-wood-products). Independently corroborated by [Forest Forward](https://forestforwardinc.ca/why-forest-forward/): "This 100% First Nation-owned company produces green lumber from nearby Lake Nipigon Forest Management Inc."
- **Location:** Sand Point First Nation / Thunder Bay, Northwestern Ontario.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin cedar and SPF boards, deck lumber and custom-cut lumber.
- **Caveat:** Supplies "contractors, developers, and communities across Ontario" — trade counter rather than pure retail.

### Leading Edge Wood Products
- **Products:** Kiln-dried lumber, timbers, beams, custom-dimensioned lumber, tongue-and-groove boards; milled and dried on site (including a Radio Frequency Kiln).
- **Ownership:** "Leading Edge is a 100% Indigenous-owned, value-added lumber company deeply rooted in the traditions of the Yunesit'in and Tsilhqot'in heritage" — [Leading Edge Wood Products, About Us](https://leadingedgewoodproducts.ca/about-us/).
- **Location:** Yunesit'in (one of six Tsilhqot'in Nation communities), along the Tsilhqox River, central British Columbia.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin timbers, beams, T&G panelling and finished lumber for home renovation projects ("premium wood products for modern homes and building projects").

### Kiskatinaw Resource Management Ltd.
- **Products:** Rough-cut lumber and firewood (split aspen and birch, up to 4 cords per dump-truck load), plus wood chips.
- **Ownership:** "Kiskatinaw Resource Management Ltd is an Indigenous owned company by one Blueberry River First Nation member and a Metis, we proudly utilize dead or downed trees to make rough cut lumber and firewood in the Treaty 8 Territory/Peace Region" — [Kiskatinaw Resource Management, About Us](https://kiskatinaw.com/about-us-1).
- **Location:** Treaty 8 Territory / Peace Region, BC; delivers to Fort St. John, Dawson Creek, Chetwynd and area.
- **Substitute for:** Imported/US firewood and rough lumber for household heating and DIY.

### Tintina Forest Products Inc.
- **Products:** Locally harvested firewood and fuelwood products for heating homes and buildings.
- **Ownership:** "Established in 2023, Tintina Forest Products, Inc. is a majority Indigenous-owned forestry business based in Dawson City, Yukon… Alice, a Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in citizen, oversees hiring, office operations, finances, and partnerships" — [Tintina Forest Products, About Us](https://www.tintina.net/about-us).
- **Location:** Dawson City, Yukon (Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in).
- **Substitute for:** Imported bagged/bulk firewood and heating fuelwood.

### Forest Forward Inc.
- **Products:** SPF glulam mass timber — beams (narrow, wide, cambered, curved, stepped), columns and one-way slabs for residential, commercial and industrial buildings.
- **Ownership:** "Forest Forward is a 100% Indigenous-owned mass timber manufacturer and research company based in Thunder Bay, Ontario (Rosslyn Village)," founded in 2023 by Cory Hubbard, "a Métis entrepreneur and mass timber researcher"; it sources SPF lumber from "100% Indigenous-owned operations" — [Forest Forward](https://forestforwardinc.ca/why-forest-forward/).
- **Location:** Rosslyn Village / Thunder Bay, Ontario.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin engineered wood / glulam beams and columns.
- **Caveat:** Project-specification (B2B) rather than shelf-retail; include only if the site covers building materials broadly.

### Waaykaash Timber Ltd.
- **Products:** Sustainably salvaged wind-fallen old-growth western red cedar ("Fallen Cedar"), log sorting, heli-logging, truck transport.
- **Ownership:** "Waaykaash Timber Ltd. is a 100% Indigenous-owned sustainable forestry company operating on Vancouver Island's West Coast… founded by father-and-son team Jordan and Erick Michael, members of the Nuchatlaht Tribe" — [Waaykaash Timber](https://waaykaashtimber.ca/).
- **Location:** Nuchatlaht traditional territory, west coast of Vancouver Island, BC.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin western red cedar.
- **Caveat:** Weakest consumer-product case in this category — the site does not list finished retail products. Include with a "raw cedar supply" label or omit.

## 2. Food & beverages (pantry, bakery, snacks, beverages, frozen and canned foods)

### St. Jean's Cannery & Smokehouse — brands: St. Jean's, Raincoast Trading, Gratitude Seafood
- **Products:** Canned and smoked wild Pacific salmon, canned albacore tuna, smoked oysters and mussels, candied salmon, smoked salmon jerky, frozen fillet portions, retort pouches, clam chowder.
- **Ownership:** "In 2015, St. Jeans ownership was transferred to the Nuu-chah-nulth, five Indigenous nations with a long and proud history as stewards and participants in BC's seafood economy" — [BC Food & Beverage Directory (Province of BC)](https://bcfooddirectory.gov.bc.ca/producer/st-jeans-cannery-smokehouse/). The owner group's own site adds: "Nuu-chah-nulth Seafood LP is a First Nation owned seafood enterprise… In November 2015, five of the Nuu-chah-nulth Seafood Nations purchased majority share in Nanaimo's St. Jean's Smokehouse and Cannery" — [Nuu-chah-nulth Seafood LP](https://www.ncnseafood.com/).
- **Location:** Nanaimo, BC (cannery); Port Alberni, BC (Nuu-chah-nulth Seafood LP office).
- **Substitute for:** US-origin canned salmon and canned tuna (Alaska/Washington packers, Bumble Bee/StarKist-type products), smoked seafood.
- **Trade framing:** Widely covered as "the last standing cannery on Canada's West Coast" and as Indigenous-owned — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10701192/bc-indigenous-cannery/).

### Wabanaki Maple
- **Products:** Barrel-aged premium maple syrup in bourbon, toasted oak and whiskey barrels; bottled and packaged by hand at the company's facility.
- **Ownership:** "Founded in 2018 in Tobique First Nation, Wabanaki Maple is a 100% Indigenous and female-owned company" — [Government of Canada / ACOA](https://www.canada.ca/en/atlantic-canada-opportunities/campaigns/impacts/wabanakimaple.html). Also: "Wabanaki Maple is a unique maple syrup company, 100% Indigenous female-owned. Located in Neqotkuk (Tobique) First Nation" — [Canadian Council for Indigenous Business member profile](https://www.ccib.ca/main/member/wabanaki-maple/).
- **Location:** Neqotkuk (Tobique) First Nation, New Brunswick.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin (Vermont/New York) maple syrup and infused syrups — the counter-tariff list covers US maple products directly.
- **Retail:** Farmers' markets, own e-commerce store, subscription boxes ([ACOA](https://www.canada.ca/en/atlantic-canada-opportunities/campaigns/impacts/wabanakimaple.html)).

### Awazibi Pure Maple Syrup
- **Products:** Pure maple syrup in 250 ml, 500 ml, 1 L bottles and 4 L jugs, no colouring or additives.
- **Ownership:** "Kitigan Zibi Anishinabeg's community owned business 'Awazibi Pure Maple Syrup' marked its first decade of operation with an expansion of the facility" — [Kitigan Zibi Natural Resources](https://www.kzanaturalresource.ca/index.php/projects/awazibi). CBC reports the First Nation "operates its own maple syrup enterprise" from roughly 16,500 taps — [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ottawa/maple-syrup-production-2022-kitigan-zibi-anishinabeg-1.6389399).
- **Location:** Kitigan Zibi Anishinabeg, near Maniwaki, Quebec.
- **Substitute for:** US maple syrup.

### Tea Horse
- **Products:** Roasted wild rice (manoomin) and tea blends — ManoominCha™ (green tea + wild rice), ManoominCha™ Dark (hojicha + wild rice), Manoominaabo™ Tisane (caffeine-free); also sells wild rice.
- **Ownership:** "Tea Horse, established in 2017 by Denise Atkinson, Anishinaabe ikwe (English: Ojibwe woman) and her partner Marc H. Bohémier, is an Indigenous-owned artisanal tea company located on the traditional territory of the Anishinaabeg Peoples in Northwestern Ontario" — [Tea Horse, About Us](https://www.teahorse.ca/about-us/). The company self-describes as a "Certified Indigenous Woman-Owned Wild Rice & Artisanal Tea Company" — [Tea Horse](https://www.teahorse.ca/).
- **Location:** Northwestern Ontario (Thunder Bay area), Anishinaabeg territory.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin packaged teas and specialty grains/rice.
- **Trade framing:** National distribution via a DAVIDsTEA collaboration described as a partnership with "a woman-led, Indigenous-owned tea company" — [DAVIDsTEA](https://davidstea.com/pages/tea-horse-partnership).

### NWC Wild Rice
- **Products:** Organic lake-harvested wild rice (including green wild rice).
- **Ownership:** "Our Indigenous-owned company takes pride in sharing this beautiful, organic food with you… Buying NWC Wild Rice means supporting us, the First Nations and Métis Harvesters, our families, and our communities" — [NWC Wild Rice, About Us](https://nwcwildrice.ca/about-us/). Harvester communities are described as Métis, Dene and Cree.
- **Location:** Northwest Saskatchewan.
- **Substitute for:** US-grown (Minnesota/California) wild rice and packaged rice.

### Pride of the Nation Maple Syrup
- **Products:** 100% Canadian maple syrup from Quebec, wood-fired evaporator process, no additives.
- **Ownership:** "Indigenous owned and operated, Pride of the Nation Maple Syrup is a unique product that has been used by Canada's Indigenous Peoples for food, medicine, and trade for many generations" — [Pride of the Nation, Our Story](https://prideofthenation.ca/pages/our-story).
- **Location:** Quebec (Érablière des Montagnes Noires / Black Mountain Maple Grove, 195-acre estate).
- **Substitute for:** US maple syrup.
- **Caveat:** Ownership stated only as "Indigenous owned and operated" on the company's own site; no nation or owner named. Weakest food-category sourcing — flag if the site requires nation-level attribution.

### Birch Bark Coffee Company
- **Products:** Five Fairtrade-certified coffee blends (e.g. Birch Bark Inukshuk, Coureurs des Bois, Summer Solstice, 2 Spirit) in ground and whole bean.
- **Ownership:** "Birch Bark Coffee Company is a First Nations–owned coffee company based in Canada," founded by Mark Marsolais-Nahwegahbow — [Birch Bark Coffee Company](https://birchbarkcoffeecompany.com/). The federal IP office describes him as "an Ojibwe Whitefish River First Nations entrepreneur" who founded the company — [Canadian Intellectual Property Office](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/corporate-information/intellectual-property-blog/birch-bark-coffee-company-making-difference-one-cup-time).
- **Location:** Ontario (roasted in Canada; founder from Whitefish River First Nation).
- **Substitute for:** US-roasted coffee brands.
- **Retail:** "Our products are stocked in select grocery stores, universities, colleges, cafes" plus direct e-commerce ([Birch Bark Coffee Company](https://birchbarkcoffeecompany.com/)).

### Spirit Bear Coffee Company
- **Products:** Organic, Fair Trade coffee — Orca dark roast, Eagle medium roast, Dolphin decaf.
- **Ownership:** "Spirit Bear Coffee Company is a locally owned & operated, First Nations business… In 2006, Sean then co-founded Spirit Bear Coffee Company, Canada's first national Indigenous owned coffee company" — [Spirit Bear Coffee Company, Founders](https://spiritbearcoffeecompany.com/pages/founders). Destination Vancouver confirms it as "the first national Indigenous-owned coffee brand," now "sold in over 600 locations nation-wide" — [Destination Vancouver](https://www.destinationvancouver.com/inspirations/food-and-drink/two-vancouver-indigenous-run-food-companies-share-a-passion-for-quality-and-community).
- **Location:** Port Coquitlam, BC.
- **Substitute for:** US-roasted coffee brands.

### First People's Coffee
- **Products:** Small-batch coffee in light, medium and dark roasts, named for locations in Kwanlin Dün First Nation traditional territory; artist-designed packaging.
- **Ownership:** "First People's Coffee is a Northern, Indigenous-owned coffee company, using our platform to tell Indigenous stories and give back to our communities" — [First People's Coffee](https://www.firstpeoplescoffee.ca/). Its launch series features Tlingit artist Mark Preston, a citizen of the Kwanlin Dün First Nation, Yukon.
- **Location:** Yukon (northern Canada).
- **Substitute for:** US-roasted coffee brands.

### Solstice Coffee (Des Nedhe Group × Road Coffee)
- **Products:** Roasted coffee — "the first coffee company in Canada to establish a fully Indigenous supply chain, connecting Indigenous growers worldwide to an Indigenous roastery in Saskatchewan… Roasted on English River First Nation Land."
- **Ownership:** Launched by Des Nedhe Group, "a diverse collective of Indigenous-owned businesses," the economic development arm tied to English River First Nation — [Solstice Coffee, Our Story](https://solsticecoffee.ca/pages/our-story).
- **Location:** English River First Nation land, Saskatchewan.
- **Substitute for:** US-roasted coffee brands.
- **Caveat:** Structured as a partnership between Des Nedhe Group (Indigenous-owned) and Road Coffee; the ownership split is not published. Flag as a joint venture.

### Tomahawk Chips (Native Canadian Chip Corporation)
- **Products:** Potato chips in five flavours — Regular, BBQ, Ketchup, Salt & Vinegar, Fire Chip — with Indigenous artists' work on the bags.
- **Ownership:** "Tomahawk chips are the brainchild of Alfred Lea, an entrepreneur from Pine Dock, Manitoba, and a member of the Fisher River Cree Nation. He created the Native Canadian Chip Corporation, which manufactures Tomahawk Chips" — [Karma Co-op](https://www.karmacoop.org/news/karmas-new-snack-tomahawk-chips-mm-2025). Global News: "The product is owned by Manitoba-based Native Canadian Chip Corporation… Having Indigenous-owned companies like this, founder Alfred Lea believes, is part of being self-reliant" — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8075350/indigenous-chip-company-tomahawk-quebec/).
- **Location:** Riverton / Pine Dock, Manitoba (Fisher River Cree Nation); manufactured in Ontario.
- **Substitute for:** US-owned snack chip brands (Frito-Lay/Lay's, Pringles) — snack foods are squarely in Canada's counter-tariff scope.
- **Retail:** Distributed across Canada in grocery and co-op retail ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8075350/indigenous-chip-company-tomahawk-quebec/)).

### Mitsoh
- **Products:** Bison pemmican strips, dry meat, smoked meat snacks and bannock-inspired products (e.g. Maple Blueberry Peppered bison pemmican, 23 g bags).
- **Ownership:** "Mitsoh is an Indigenous owned Canadian food company that specializes in traditional pemmican, dry meat, and bannock inspired products… Founded in 2018 by Ian Gladue of Alberta's Bigstone Cree Nation" — [Jerky Ingredients product review](https://jerkyingredients.com/2026/06/09/mitsoh-maple-blueberry-peppered-bison-pemmican-strip/).
- **Location:** Beaumont, Alberta (Treaty Six Territory); founder from Bigstone Cree Nation.
- **Substitute for:** US-owned meat snack brands (Jack Link's, Slim Jim) — jerky/meat snacks are a US-import category.
- **Retail:** Sold in retail health-food chains (e.g. Healthy Planet, Brampton ON) ([Jerky Ingredients](https://jerkyingredients.com/2026/06/09/mitsoh-maple-blueberry-peppered-bison-pemmican-strip/)).

### Bangin' Bannock
- **Products:** Shelf-stable bannock/frybread dry mixes — OG Bannock Mix ($11.99), Gluten-Free Bannock Mix ($13.99), Bulk Bannock Mix ($39.99).
- **Ownership:** "Bangin' Bannock is an Indigenous-owned, woman-powered enterprise" — [Bangin' Bannock, About Us](https://banginbannock.ca/pages/about-us). Founders are named as Kelsey Coutts (father Nak'azdli Dakelh) and Destiny Houshte, "a registered treaty member of White Bear First Nation in Southern Saskatchewan in Treaty 4 Territory."
- **Location:** Vancouver, BC (unceded Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-Waututh territories); co-founder registered to White Bear First Nation, SK.
- **Substitute for:** US-owned baking mixes (Bisquick-type pancake/biscuit mixes) — a counter-tariffed bakery/pantry category.
- **Retail:** "Find our delicious products at a variety of locations across Canada. From local cafes to eco-friendly markets" ([Bangin' Bannock](https://banginbannock.ca/)).

### The Red River Bannock Company
- **Products:** Premixed Bannock Dry Mix made with local organic ingredients; Bannock & Tea gift set.
- **Ownership:** "A Metis owned and operated company. I am proud to share my Metis culture with you by offering a premixed Bannock Dry Mix" — [The Red River Bannock Company](https://redriverbannock.ca/).
- **Location:** Red River / Manitoba region (city not stated on site).
- **Substitute for:** US-owned baking and pancake mixes.

### Raven & Hummingbird Tea Co.
- **Products:** Small-batch loose-leaf Indigenous herbal tea blends; also salves, tinctures and beeswax lip products.
- **Ownership:** "Raven & Hummingbird Tea Co. is a Squamish Nation–owned Indigenous herbal tea company led by a multigenerational family: T'uy't'tanat Cease Wyss, Sen̓áḵw Senaqwila Wyss, and Justin Leo… Cease and Senaqwila Wyss are proud members of the Squamish Nation" — [Raven & Hummingbird Tea Co.](https://rhtea.co/).
- **Location:** Squamish Nation territory / Lower Mainland, BC.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin packaged herbal teas.

### Boreal Heartland Herbal Products Inc.
- **Products:** Boreal teas (fermented fireweed & wild mint, fireweed, Labrador tea), dried wild mushrooms, herbs, and Firestix fire starters.
- **Ownership:** "Boreal Heartland Herbal Products Inc is Indigenous owned and operated, began in 2017 and is based in Air Ronge, SK, Canada. It is an initiative of Keewatin Community Development Association" — [Waboos Indigenous business directory](https://waboos.ca/business/boreal_heartland/). Company site: "Premium quality teas, mushrooms and herbs sustainably harvested by Indigenous people from the boreal forest of northern Saskatchewan" — [Boreal Heartland](https://borealheartland.ca/).
- **Location:** Air Ronge, northern Saskatchewan.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin packaged teas and dried food ingredients.
- **Retail:** "Our products are in retail stores across Canada and in Coop Food Stores in Western Canada" ([Boreal Heartland](https://borealheartland.ca/)).
- **Caveat:** The ownership statement comes from a third-party Indigenous business directory, not the company's own site — slightly weaker than the rest of this list.

### FN Clean Water
- **Products:** Bottled mineral-enhanced drinking water (cases; local Ottawa delivery and Canada Post shipping).
- **Ownership:** "100% Indigenous-owned… Tyson Wesley is Cree and raised in Kashechewan First Nation, he is also from Waskaganish on his father's side. Natasha Commanda is Algonquin and Ojibway from Kitigan Zibi, and from Grassy Narrows on her mother's side" — [FN Clean Water, Our Story](https://fncleanwater.ca/pages/about-us).
- **Location:** Ottawa, Ontario.
- **Substitute for:** US-bottled water brands (Dasani, Aquafina, Poland Spring) — bottled water is on Canada's counter-tariff list.
- **Extra:** "Portion of proceeds go to Water First to support First Nations."

### Nation Water
- **Products:** Bottled spring water sourced from an Alberta foothills aquifer.
- **Ownership:** "our 100% Indigenous owned and operated Nation Water will leave you feeling great at every level" — [Nation Water](https://nationspringwater.com/).
- **Location:** Alberta foothills, at the base of the Canadian Rockies.
- **Substitute for:** US-bottled water brands.
- **Caveat:** No nation or owner named on the site; ownership stated as 100% Indigenous only.

### Arctic Beverages
- **Products:** Bottled/canned soft drinks (Pepsi portfolio) plus distribution of Nestlé ice cream, Frito Lay, Grupo Bimbo, meat snacks and South Cove confectionery.
- **Ownership:** "In 1991 Arctic Beverages became the first and only First Nations owned and operated Pepsi Bottler in Northern Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Northern Ontario, Nunavut, and Whitehorse" — [Arctic Beverages](https://arcticbev.com/).
- **Location:** Northern Manitoba, serving northern MB/SK/ON, Nunavut and Whitehorse.
- **Substitute for:** Not a brand-owner substitute — it bottles and distributes licensed US-owned brands. **Recommend excluding** from a "choose Canadian instead of American" list; noted here for completeness because it is a genuine First Nations–owned beverage manufacturer.

## 3. Clothing, footwear and textiles

### Victoria's Arctic Fashion (V.A.F.)
- **Products:** Modernized Inuit parkas, northern garments and accessories using traditional fabrics, furs and skins.
- **Ownership:** "Started by Victoria Kakuktinniq… The creator of V.A.F., Victoria Kakuktinniq, is an Inuk woman, born and raised in Rankin Inlet, Nunavut" — [Victoria's Arctic Fashion, About Us](https://vafashion.ca/pages/about-us).
- **Location:** Rankin Inlet, Nunavut.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin winter outerwear/parkas (The North Face, Columbia, Patagonia — all US-headquartered).
- **Retail:** "V.A.F. designs are sold throughout Canada and Greenland."

### Bastien Industries
- **Products:** Moccasins and mukluks — 225+ handcrafted models including the Hiawatha line, moose-hide classics and suede silhouettes, made with 100% genuine Canadian leather.
- **Ownership:** "Bastien Industries… is an Indigenous-owned business headquartered in Wendake, Québec, Canada, Est. 1972… founded in 1972 by Roland Bastien in Wendake, a self-governing Huron-Wendat territory north of Québec City. Today, under the leadership of Jason Picard-Binet — a proud member of the Huron-Wendat Nation — Bastien Industries continues its legacy" — [Bastien Industries](https://www.bastienmoccasins.com/).
- **Location:** Wendake, Quebec (Huron-Wendat Nation).
- **Substitute for:** US-origin slippers and casual footwear (Minnetonka, L.L.Bean, UGG).
- **Trade framing:** Company mission is "to offer authentic, made-in-Canada Huron-Wendat products."

### SECTION 35
- **Products:** Streetwear apparel and headwear.
- **Ownership:** "SECTION 35 is an Indigenous owned streetwear brand based on the Unceded Territories of the Xʷməθkʷəy̓əm (Musqueam), Sḵwx̱wú7mesh (Squamish), and Sel̓íl̓witulh (Tsleil-Waututh) Nations… Justin Jacob Louis is the Creative Director and Founder of SECTION 35. He is a member of the Samson Cree Nation" — [SECTION 35, About](https://www.sectionthirtyfive.com/pages/about).
- **Location:** Vancouver, BC; founder from Nipisihkopahk (Samson Cree Nation), Treaty 6.
- **Substitute for:** US streetwear/apparel brands (Nike, Champion, Supreme).
- **Retail:** Carried by Foot Locker Canada's Home Grown program — described as "Indigenous-owned streetwear brand Section 35" — [StyleDemocracy](https://www.styledemocracy.com/indigenous-owned-streetwear-brand-enters-foot-locker-canada/).

### Anne Mulaire
- **Products:** Sustainable, size-inclusive nature-inspired clothing collections and custom clothing, each piece made in Winnipeg.
- **Ownership:** "Driven by her respect for people and the planet, Andréanne (Anne) Mulaire Dandeneau founded the sustainable and size-inclusive fashion brand, Anne Mulaire. Rooted in her Anishinaabe/ French Métis from the Red River Nation on treaty 1, Anne creates nature-inspired clothing on Treaty 1 territory" — [Anne Mulaire, Our Story](https://annemulaire.ca/pages/our-story). The National Indigenous Economic Development Board describes it as "an award-winning fashion brand established by Red River Métis designer Andréanne (Anne) Mulaire Dandeneau" — [NIEDB](https://www.niedb-cndea.ca/success-stories/anne-mulaire-sustainable-fashion-with-metis-roots/).
- **Location:** Winnipeg, Manitoba (Treaty 1, Red River Métis).
- **Substitute for:** US-origin athleisure and basics (Lululemon is Canadian, but Gap, Athleta, Old Navy, Hanes are US).

### NEECHI By Nature Inc.
- **Products:** Apparel (men's and gender-neutral), accessories, footwear, athleisure.
- **Ownership:** "Founded in 2020, Neechi By Nature Inc. is a Toronto-based Indigenous-owned brand that bridges tradition and urban identity through innovative apparel, accessories, footwear, art, and entertainment… Shane Kejick is the co-founder and creative force behind NEECHI By Nature, an Indigenous-owned fashion and art brand based in Toronto" — [NEECHI By Nature, Our Story](https://neechibynature.com/pages/our-story-about-us). The founder's work "reflects his Anishinaabe heritage."
- **Location:** Toronto, Ontario.
- **Substitute for:** US streetwear and apparel brands.

### Red Rebel Armour Inc.
- **Products:** Tees, hoodies, crewnecks, headwear, joggers, shorts, pants, youth apparel (e.g. Every Child Matters Tee $30, Protection Path Hoodie $70.99) — [Red Rebel Armour](https://redrebelarmour.ca/).
- **Ownership:** "Red Rebel Armour was founded by Sean Rayland-Boubar, an Anishinaabe entrepreneur from Winnipeg" — [Carleton University Centre for Indigenous Support & Community Engagement](https://carleton.ca/indigenous/cisce/people/red-rebel-armour/). Also described as "an Indigenous-owned streetwear and social enterprise based in Winnipeg" — [CanadianSME](https://canadiansme.ca/indigenous-entrepreneur-of-the-year-2025/).
- **Location:** Winnipeg, Manitoba.
- **Substitute for:** US-origin T-shirts, hoodies and printed apparel.

### MINI TIPI
- **Products:** Reversible wool-blend blankets (60"×80", $198), reversible shawls ($134), mittens, and Thunderbird boots (a collaboration with Bastien).
- **Ownership:** Brand self-describes as "Proudly Indigenous"; "Founded in 2016 by Mélanie Bernard and Trisha Pitura. MINI TIPI's aim is to address the lack of authentic Indigenous designs in the textile industry" — [MINI TIPI](https://www.minitipi.ca/). The Women Entrepreneurship Knowledge Hub states: "Trisha Pitura and Mélanie Bernard are the founders of MINI TIPI, a brand of blankets and accessories for adults and children. Their designs reflect their Indigenous heritage, and are designed and produced in Quebec" — [WEKH](https://wekh.ca/entrepreneurs/trisha-pitura/).
