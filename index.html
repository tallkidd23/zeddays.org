How personal avoidance adds pressure

One choice feels small. Together, they add up.

When you choose a product made in Canada, or somewhere other than the U.S., one fewer American product is sold. One choice is small. When many Canadians make the same choice, fewer orders reach U.S. businesses. That gives those businesses a reason to push for a change in U.S. trade policy.

  1. 01 Start with one everyday choice.

    Choose something Canadian, look for another country of origin, buy used, repair, or simply wait.

  2. 02 That choice changes what gets sold.

    When the same products keep getting passed over, retailers and importers may begin ordering less of them.

  3. 03 Small choices begin to add up.

    Across many households, fewer purchases can become a noticeable shift in orders, inventory and sales.

  4. 04 Businesses start paying attention.

    As Canadian demand shifts, affected U.S. suppliers have a reason to speak up and push for change.

Illustrative example A U.S.-made furniture purchase
1 household C$1,000 of U.S.-origin demand avoided

Simple multiplication for illustration, not a forecast. It assumes each household avoids C$1,000 in U.S.-origin purchases. The tariff itself is assessed on customs value, not necessarily the store price. Check the product’s country of origin, not only its brand.

Method & limits

What this dashboard measures

  • Rows are parsed from the Government of Canada consolidated schedule updated August 26, 2026.
  • Sector labels are analytical groupings based primarily on HS chapter, with product-term rules for appliances and farm equipment.
  • The government reports C$27.6B in covered U.S. imports for the measure as a whole. It does not publish a value beside each tariff item in this schedule.
  • “Trade impact” therefore remains “aggregate only” at item level. Sector bars compare tariff-line breadth, not dollars.
  • The 648-line total is authoritative for the current September 8 table. Historical or expired countermeasures are excluded.
  • The Top 10 is an editorial action list. It prioritizes consumer-facing products using rate, visibility, substitutability and covered-line breadth. It is not presented as a dollar ranking.
